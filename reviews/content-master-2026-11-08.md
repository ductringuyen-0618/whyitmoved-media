## Duplicates and patterns found

1. Three rank-card videos in 3 days (11-05, 11-06, 11-07): hook + 4-5 rank cards + outro, same card layout, same "counted down" wording, same "surprised you most" question. Playbook D says never the same hook-to-outro sequence as either of the last 2 posts and never the same format in the last 3 posts; the ranking runs treated "ranking" as a format that only they use, so it was not rotated. Weekly was then forced into the same look.
2. "Headline vs expectation" lesson repeated 4 times: SHOP outro, QCOM preview outro, ranking outro ("followed what companies said"), explainer outro. Different words, same idea.
3. Comment question "Did the market overreact?" on PLTR, ORCL, SHOP and (as variants) "surprised you most" on 3 rankings: 7 of 12 captions with a question use one of two stems. They drew 0-1 comments each.
4. Caption opener "<Co> stock fell/jumped N% ... <emoji>": 10 of 14 posts.
5. Title formula "Why <Co> Stock ... ?": 8 of 14 titles contain "Why".
6. Story repeats: Shopify in SHOP, the 11-03 caption and the explainer (3 videos in 6 days); AppLovin in 11-05 (#2), 11-06 (#1), weekly (#2); AMD in the selloff post, the catalyst video and the 11-06 ranking.
7. Catalyst pair AMD / RIOT share beat sequence (hook, headline, chain, stat or shift, outro) and the "so far" device; fine as a format, but two in one day with the same label.
8. Layout: every format uses the same dark card, top-heavy composition with the lower third of the frame empty (11-08 explainer and 11-07 weekly sheets show ~25-35% of the frame below the tape with nothing). The video still reads as a card deck, not a native short.
9. Over-used words: "big" (big moves, big beat), "counted down", "so far", "surprised you most", "Here's why".

## Top 5 ranked changes (exact wording)

1. Rotate away from rank cards. Next 7 days: at most 1 ranking and no weekly with the same hook+rank skeleton. Replace the 11-05/11-06 style with a "one story from the list" video. Example hook for a 5-mover day: "FIS fell 24% after one sentence about next year. Here's the sentence." Evidence: three rank-card videos in 3 days, 111-192 views each vs 319-534 for single-story surprise videos (weak, confounded).
2. Fix the first 8 seconds. Put the answer's first clause by second 5, and cap every beat at 8 s (weekly rank beats are 19-27 words, ~8-10 s each). Example: weekly beat "Number four. Amdocs fell 30%. Its biggest customer fight got worse." (11 words) instead of the 24-word version. Evidence: YouTube average view duration 6-12 s.
3. Replace the comment question with real forks, and rotate by video. Example: "Good news that was already priced in: fair drop or overreaction? A or B." Evidence: "Did the market overreact?" and "surprised you most?" drew 0-1 comments on 7 posts.
4. Stop recycling stories. Anti-repeat rule: a company gets a second video in 7 days only with a new catalyst; explainers should use a company not featured in the last 7 days. Example: next explainer "What is a guidance cut?" using FIS or DraftKings, not Shopify.
5. Title and caption variety: alternate "Why X ..." with contrast titles ("Beat on Sales, Lost 6%: Shopify's Catch") and open captions on the stakes not the percentage ("$1B under estimates. That was the whole story."). Evidence: 8 of 14 titles contain "Why", 10 of 14 openers are "<Co> stock fell/jumped N%".
