# Do not reuse this week (Content Master, 2026-11-08)

Read by every production run. Do not reuse anything below; use the fresh alternatives. Replaced every Sunday.

## Hook patterns (retire)
- "X led N big moves. K of them fell/went up." (used 11-05 and 11-06 back to back). Try: open on the odd one out ("One stock fell 17% the day it beat estimates"), or a question about the number ("Why did a 55% sales jump not save it?").
- "X fell/jumped N% on <day>, even though/after Y." as sentence one. Try: stakes first ("A $28B lease. Was it priced in by 10 a.m.?"), a before/after pair, or a plain-word "what would you guess?" setup.
- "Five tech stocks. 25%+ in one day." / "counted down" / "Five big moves, counted down". Try: no countdown language; name the shared thread ("Three of these five did the same thing before they jumped").

## Outro lessons (retire)
- Anything saying the headline is not what matters: "markets react to the number they were watching, not the headline", "look past the headline number", "good news helps most when it beats what's expected". Three variants of one idea in 6 days. Try: money-flow lessons ("A raise you pay for in shares costs holders", "Cash in the bank beats a plan on paper"), time lessons ("Premarket is a draft, the close is the answer"), or sizing lessons ("A 30% drop on a $5B company is not a $5B event").
- "Early pops can shrink", "a signed lease is firmer than a plan", "company news moves one stock, a selloff moves many". Fine ideas, used once each; do not repeat.
- Every outro ending "And that's why" on a loop is fine, but vary the sentence length and make the last words the rule itself.

## Title formulas (retire)
- "Why <Company> Stock Fell/Jumped N%" (4 of 14 titles) and "...: Why?" suffix (3 of 14). Try: the contrast ("Beat on Sales, Lost 6%: Shopify's Catch"), the number-as-question ("What Did Riot Just Sign for $28B?"), the rule ("Good News Can Still Sink a Stock").
- "N Big Stock Moves, Ranked" and "The 5 Biggest ... This Week". Try a theme title ("Three Stocks, One Mistake").

## Caption openers (retire)
- "<Company> stock fell/jumped N% ..." + one emoji as line 1 (10 of 14). Try opening with the plain-word question or the number's context ("$1B under estimates. That's all it took."), keep the emoji optional.
- "Two went up and three fell." / "Which of the four surprised you most?"

## Comment questions (retire)
- "Did the market overreact? Tell me below" (4 posts, 0-1 comments each). "Which move surprised you most?" (3 posts, ~0 comments). "Was this one on your radar?" Try real either/or forks: "Fair or overreaction: A) a 6% drop B) should have been bigger. A or B?", "Would you call this good news or bad news? Pick one and say why in 5 words.", "Name a stock that fell on good news. Best one gets a breakdown."

## Words and phrases (retire)
- "counted down", "big moves" / "big beat", "surprised you most", "so far" as a filler (keep only when sourced intraday), "priced high", "Here's why". 
- Number-count sameness: do not open with "five/four" countdowns twice in a week.

## Stories and angles (rest these)
- Shopify (3 videos in 6 days incl. the explainer), AppLovin (3 videos: 11-05 #2, 11-06 #1, weekly #2), AMD (3). Do not feature any of them again this week unless there is a NEW catalyst.
- "Beat but fell" story arc: used for PLTR, SHOP, QCOM, explainer. Try "missed but rose", "good news from a rival hits a stock", or "what the CEO actually said on the call".

## Layout/format
- Never run ranking / weekly rank beats more than twice in 7 days (11-05, 11-06, 11-07 were three in a row, all hook + rank cards + outro).
